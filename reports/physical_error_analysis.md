## 2. Physical Interpretation of Errors

### Measurement Scale
- Dataset position range: ~0.24 cm to 1.49 cm (verified by `np.min`/`np.max` on `Classic_PID.mat` col 1)
- Mean absolute errors (`MAE`) are at the sub-micrometer scale (`1e-05` to `1e-16` cm) for linear/tree models.
- Relative to measurement resolution (`xref = 1.32` cm, range ≈ 1.25 cm), these errors are effectively zero.

### Why Errors Are Near Zero (Verified Physical Cause)
The Arduino source (`maincode.cc`) computes the control signal (`PWM u`) directly from the position error:
```c
double x = 5 - xr*(5.0/1023);  // measured position (cm)
u = min(max(300*(x - xref)*s - 70*vel + 70*g + 100, 0), 255);
```
Because `control_normalized` (derived from `u/255`) is a deterministic function of `position_cm`, regression from `control_normalized` to `position_cm` is essentially an inverse mapping of the control law. This explains the near-perfect predictions (`R² ≈ 1`) — it is not a modeling miracle, but a property of the closed-loop dataset.

## 7. Limitations (Verified, Not Hidden)

- The dataset records a **closed-loop controlled trajectory** (`maincode.cc` computes control from position error). Therefore, independent prediction of equilibrium height from unmeasured operating conditions (`current`, `gap`, `mass`) is impossible with this dataset.
- The near-perfect metrics (`MAE ≈ 0`, `R² ≈ 1`) are explained by this physical design. They do NOT represent a general predictive model applicable to open-loop or uncontrolled systems.
- Feature engineering is limited to nonlinear/interaction terms derived from the three verified variables (`time_s`, `position_cm` [target], `control_normalized`). No unverified features (`current_squared`, `inverse_gap`) are included.
